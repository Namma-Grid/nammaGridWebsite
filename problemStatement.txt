AI for EV Charging Optimization & InfrastructurePlanning by BESCOM

Context

Electric vehicle (EV) adoption in Bengaluru is growing rapidly, creating new andconcentrated demand on the power distribution network. Charging demand is often clusteredin specific locations and time windows—especially during evening hours—leading tolocalized grid stress.At the same time, EV charging infrastructure is still evolving. Decisions on where to installnew charging stations and how to manage charging behavior are not alwaysdata-driven.BESCOM needs systems that can:

anticipate EV-driven demand,
optimize when charging happens, and
guide where charging infrastructure should be built.
The solution must operate as a decision-support layer without changing existing systems.


The Problem

Build an AI-based system to:

Part A — EV Charging Demand & Scheduling

predict EV charging demand patterns (time and location)
recommend optimal charging times to reduce peak load
align charging behavior with grid conditions (e.g., off-peak periods)
Part B — Charging Infrastructure Location Planning

identify high-demand zones for EV charging
recommend optimal locations for new charging stations
consider factors such as demand growth, existing infrastructure, and load capacity
 

Non-Negotiables

No modification to existing distribution systems
Works as a decision-support layer
Uses masked or synthetic data where required
Outputs must be explainable and actionable
Recommendations should consider grid constraints
No hosted LLM usage on sensitive data
What Success Looks Like

A successful solution enables:

Prediction of EV charging demand by time and location
Charging schedules that reduce peak load impact
Identification of priority zones for new charging infrastructure
Recommendations aligned with grid capacity and demand patterns
Clear, actionable insights for planners and operators
 

Sample Scenario

Data on EV adoption, charging behavior (real or simulated), and grid demand patterns isavailable across city zones.A good solution would:

predict when and where EV charging demand will rise
recommend shifting charging to off-peak hours
identify corridors or clusters with growing EV demand
suggest locations for new charging stations
For example:

evening charging spikes in residential areas → recommend delayed charging
rising EV density in a corridor → flag for new charging infrastructure
Outputs should be comparable against baseline approaches such as uniform infrastructureplacement or unmanaged charging
